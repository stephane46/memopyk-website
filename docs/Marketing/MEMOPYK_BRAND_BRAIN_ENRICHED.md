## 1. Brand Identity

**Key**: `brand_identity`

```

WHAT WE DON'T DO: We are NOT a photo editing/retouching service (that's photALL, our sister company). We don't work on individual images. We create complete narrative experiences from entire collections.

SERVICE DETAILS: Films on custom quotation starting from €150. Includes 2 rounds of revisions. 50% deposit to start, 50% on final approval. Delivery in 1-3 weeks. Payment via bank transfer, Stripe, or PayPal.

MARKETS: Primary — France (French-speaking). Secondary — English-speaking (US, Canada, international). All content produced bilingually.

BRAND RELATIONSHIP: MEMOPYK is the consumer-facing brand. photALL is the professional photo retouching brand. They share the same founder but serve different markets with different services.
```

## 2. Tone & Voice

**Key**: `tone_voice`

```
CORE VOICE: Warm, personal, emotionally intelligent. We write like a trusted friend who happens to be a professional — knowledgeable but never cold or corporate.

EMOTIONAL REGISTER:
- We treat memories with reverence — they are irreplaceable treasures, not "content"
- We acknowledge the emotional weight: nostalgia, love, loss, joy, the passage of time
- We understand the frustration of media chaos without being condescending about it
- We celebrate life's milestones: births, weddings, travel, family reunions, even pet adventures
- For memorial/bereavement content: gentle, respectful, never exploitative. Honor the grief while showing how preserving memories can bring comfort.

LANGUAGE RULES:
- French: Always use "vous" (formal). Never "tu."
- French: Prefer "film souvenir" as our core product term
- English: Prefer "souvenir film" or "memory film." Use "slideshow" only when targeting that search intent — our product is more than a slideshow
- Both languages: Use "we/nous" — we are a team, even if small. Feels more trustworthy than "I"
- Both: Use active voice. "We sort your photos" not "Your photos are sorted"

DO:
- Use sensory and emotional language: "relive," "cherish," "rediscover," "treasure"
- Paint pictures: "that photo of your daughter's first steps, buried in a folder labeled IMG_4032"
- Acknowledge the reader's real situation: overwhelmed, time-poor, emotionally attached to their memories
- Be encouraging: "No need to sort anything — that's our job"
- Use concrete examples from real life scenarios (travel, baby's first year, wedding, retirement, pet)

DON'T:
- Use corporate jargon: "leverage," "optimize," "deliverables," "synergy"
- Use aggressive sales language: "Act now!" "Limited time!" "Don't miss out!"
- Be preachy about the importance of memories — show it, don't lecture
- Use tech jargon unless explaining a process: "cloud storage," "resolution," "bitrate"
- Overuse exclamation marks
- Sound robotic or template-generated — every post should feel like it was written by someone who cares
```

## 3. Writing Rules

**Key**: `writing_rules`

```
BLOG POST STRUCTURE:
- Length: 800-1500 words for standard posts. Up to 2000 for pillar/guide articles.
- Title: Include primary keyword. Make it compelling and specific, not generic.
- Introduction (100-150 words): Hook the reader emotionally or with a relatable scenario. State the primary keyword naturally in the first paragraph. Set up what the article will deliver.
- Body: Use H2 subheadings every 2-3 paragraphs. Each section should deliver standalone value.
- Conclusion (80-120 words): Summarize key takeaway. Transition naturally to CTA.

CALL-TO-ACTION RULES:
- Every post MUST include at least one CTA to MEMOPYK's service
- Primary CTA options: "Book a free consultation" / "Request a personalized quote" / "Réserver une consultation gratuite" / "Obtenir un devis personnalisé"
- CTA placement: one mid-article (soft, contextual), one at the end (direct)
- For gift-related content: position MEMOPYK as "the perfect personalized gift" — a souvenir film made from their photos
- For DIY/tool content: position MEMOPYK as the professional alternative when DIY becomes frustrating or time-consuming
- For memorial content: gentle CTA — "When you're ready, we're here to help preserve those memories"
- Never force a CTA where it feels unnatural

INTERNAL LINKING:
- Link to related blog posts when they exist (the system provides published posts context)
- Link to the Gallery page when mentioning examples or pricing
- Link to the FAQ when answering a common question in passing
- Use descriptive anchor text, not "click here"

CONTENT TYPES & ANGLES:
- Gift guides: "Best [occasion] gift ideas" → list format, MEMOPYK positioned as a standout option among others
- How-to/DIY: "How to create a [type] slideshow" → helpful tutorial, with honest mention that a pro service saves time and delivers better results
- Emotional/storytelling: "Why preserving memories matters for [audience]" → narrative-driven, personal
- Comparison: "DIY tools vs. professional service" → fair comparison, highlight where MEMOPYK excels (handling chaos, narrative craft, zero effort for client)
- Life event: "Celebrating [event] with a souvenir film" → scenario-based, relatable

FORMATTING:
- Use bold sparingly — only for key terms or phrases that aid scanning
- Use bullet lists only when listing items (gift ideas, steps, features) — not for regular prose
- Include a meta description under 160 characters
- Include alt text suggestions for any images referenced
```

## 4. Target Audience

**Key**: `target_audience`

```
PRIMARY SEGMENTS:

1. MEMORY-OVERWHELMED FAMILIES (largest segment)
   - Age: 35-65
   - Situation: Thousands of photos/videos accumulated over years across phones, hard drives, old computers, boxes of prints
   - Pain: "I keep meaning to organize our photos but never have time." Guilt about neglecting memories.
   - Trigger events: child's milestone birthday, wedding anniversary, retirement, moving house, aging parent
   - What they value: Quality, emotion, someone handling the chaos for them
   - Objection: "Is it worth the money?" → Show the emotional ROI and time saved

2. GIFT GIVERS (high-intent, often seasonal)
   - Age: 25-55
   - Situation: Looking for a meaningful, personal, original gift — not another generic item
   - Occasions: Retirement, wedding anniversary, Mother's/Father's Day, Christmas, birthday milestone, memorial/tribute
   - Pain: "I want to give something truly special and personal"
   - What they value: Uniqueness, emotional impact, the "wow factor" when the recipient watches
   - Approach: Position the souvenir film as "the gift they'll watch again and again"

3. ADVENTURE/TRAVEL ENTHUSIASTS
   - Age: 25-45
   - Situation: Came back from an epic trip with hundreds of GoPro clips and phone photos
   - Pain: Raw footage is unwatchable. No time or skill to edit a proper film.
   - What they value: Dynamic editing, social-media-ready formats, preserving the achievement
   - Approach: Show examples like the mountain bike and safari films

4. BEREAVED/MEMORIAL SEEKERS (sensitive segment — mostly US/EN market)
   - Situation: Lost a loved one (or pet), want to create a tribute video from their photos
   - Pain: Emotionally overwhelmed, not capable of doing this themselves right now
   - What they value: Sensitivity, professionalism, someone trustworthy handling precious memories of someone they've lost
   - Approach: Always gentle. Never salesy. "When you're ready" language. Focus on honoring and preserving.

5. PROFESSIONAL PHOTOGRAPHERS (B2B partnership channel)
   - Situation: Photographers who shoot weddings, events, family sessions — their clients want more than just delivered photos
   - Opportunity: MEMOPYK as a white-label or partnership service. Photographer earns commission (20% first order, 10% recurring).
   - Approach: Frame as added revenue stream and client retention tool

PSYCHOGRAPHIC NOTES:
- Our audience values quality over speed, emotion over efficiency
- They are NOT tech-savvy editors — that's why they need us
- They often feel nostalgic, sentimental, or even guilty about neglected memories
- They are willing to pay for expertise but need to understand what they're paying for (hence custom quotes + gallery examples)
- Life events are the #1 trigger — content should align with these moments
```

## 5. SEO Guidelines

**Key**: `seo_guidelines`

```
ON-PAGE SEO RULES:

TITLE (H1):
- Include primary keyword, ideally near the beginning
- Keep under 60 characters for SERP display
- Make it compelling — not just keyword-stuffed. "Cadeau Retraite Femme : 15 Idées Qui Touchent le Cœur" > "Cadeau Retraite Femme"

META DESCRIPTION:
- Under 155 characters
- Include primary keyword naturally
- Include a value proposition or emotional hook
- End with an implicit or explicit CTA when appropriate

HEADINGS:
- H1: One per page (the title)
- H2: Major sections. Include secondary keywords where natural.
- H3: Subsections within H2s if needed. Don't force keyword placement here.

KEYWORD USAGE:
- Primary keyword in: title, first paragraph, one H2, meta description, URL slug
- Secondary keywords: sprinkle naturally throughout body text — aim for 2-3 mentions each
- Related/LSI keywords: use naturally to build topical authority
- Never keyword-stuff. If it reads awkwardly, rephrase.
- For French: include both accented and common unaccented variants naturally (e.g., both "idée cadeau" and the natural flow of the text will cover "idee cadeau" queries)

URL SLUG:
- Short, lowercase, hyphenated, keyword-rich
- French example: /blog/cadeau-retraite-femme
- English example: /blog/retirement-gift-ideas-women

INTERNAL LINKING:
- Every post should link to at least 1-2 other blog posts
- Pillar pages link to all their spoke articles
- Spoke articles link back to their pillar
- Use keyword-rich anchor text

CONTENT STRUCTURE FOR SEO:
- Hub-and-spoke model: pillar articles (comprehensive guides, 1500-2000 words) link to spoke articles (focused topics, 800-1200 words)
- Each spoke targets a specific long-tail keyword cluster
- Pillar targets the broad head term

IMAGE SEO:
- Alt text describing the image with keyword context where natural
- Filename should be descriptive (souvenir-film-mariage.jpg not IMG_4032.jpg)

MARKET-SPECIFIC:
- French content: target .fr searches, French-Canadian where applicable
- English content: target US market primarily (larger volume, "slideshow" preferred over "montage")
- Memorial/sympathy content is a much bigger opportunity in EN/US than in FR
- VHS/digitization keywords have strong search volume — use as traffic magnets with conversion path to MEMOPYK service
```

## 6. Translation Rules

**Key**: `translation_rules`

```
GENERAL PRINCIPLES:
- Translate meaning and emotion, not word-for-word. The French and English versions should feel like they were each written natively.
- Preserve the same structure, heading hierarchy, and formatting between languages.
- Keep the same CTA intent but adapt the wording to sound natural in each language.
- Do NOT translate brand names: MEMOPYK, photALL stay as-is.

TERMINOLOGY MAPPING (maintain consistency):
- "film souvenir" (FR) ↔ "souvenir film" or "memory film" (EN)
- "montage vidéo" (FR) ↔ "slideshow" or "video montage" (EN) — prefer "slideshow" for US SEO, "video montage" when describing our premium service
- "diaporama" (FR) ↔ "slideshow" (EN)
- "devis" (FR) ↔ "quote" or "quotation" (EN)
- "consultation gratuite" (FR) ↔ "free consultation" (EN)
- "sur mesure" (FR) ↔ "custom-made" or "bespoke" (EN)
- "séance de consultation" (FR) ↔ "consultation session" (EN)
- "retouche photo" (FR) ↔ "photo retouching" (EN)
- "cadeau" (FR) ↔ "gift" (EN)
- "deuil / hommage / condoléances" (FR) ↔ "memorial / tribute / sympathy" (EN)
- € (FR) ↔ USD or $ (EN) — adjust currency symbol but do NOT convert amounts unless specifically asked

FRENCH-SPECIFIC:
- Always "vous" — never "tu"
- Use "nous" for the company voice
- Preserve French punctuation rules: space before : ; ? !
- Use « guillemets » for quotes in French
- Keep accent marks correct: é, è, ê, ë, à, â, ç, ù, û, ô, î

ENGLISH-SPECIFIC:
- Use American English spelling: "personalized" not "personalised," "organize" not "organise"
- Use American date format references in content (Month Day, Year)
- Cultural adaptation: American readers relate more to "slideshow" culture; memorial/sympathy gifting is a larger market in the US
- "Souvenir film" may need context for US readers — occasionally explain: "a professionally edited film made from your own photos and videos"

SEO ADAPTATION:
- Each language version targets its own keyword set — do NOT simply translate keywords
- French primary keyword and English primary keyword are provided separately in the post configuration
- Adapt meta descriptions independently for each language to match respective keyword targets
- URL slugs are created independently per language

CULTURAL REFERENCES:
- Adapt holiday references: "Fête des Mères" → "Mother's Day," "Noël" → "Christmas"
- Adapt life event framing: French "pot de départ" has no direct English equivalent — use "retirement party" or "farewell celebration"
- Gift-giving customs differ: adapt gift guide framing to local norms
```
